Sleep and Recovery for Young Athletes: How Much Is Enough?

Sleep is the most underrated part of youth sport performance. How much kids and teens need and simple habits that improve it.

TSC Academy coaching team · November 20, 2026 · 1 min read

If there were a supplement that improved reaction time, mood, learning and injury resistance at the same time, every parent would buy it. It exists, and it's free: sleep.

How much sleep do they need?

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine, with support from the American Academy of Pediatrics, recommends:

  • Ages 6–12: 9 to 12 hours per 24 hours
  • Ages 13–18: 8 to 10 hours per 24 hours

Many teen athletes get far less, between early school starts, evening training, homework and phones.

Why it matters for performance

  • Learning skills. The brain consolidates new movements during sleep.
  • Recovery and growth. Much of the body's repair happens at night.
  • Focus and decision-making are worse after short nights.
  • Mood and motivation drop when kids are chronically tired.
  • Injury risk tends to be higher in athletes who sleep less.

Simple habits that help

  1. 1. Same wake-up time most days, including weekends where possible.
  2. 2. Screens out of the bedroom, or off 30–60 minutes before bed.
  3. 3. Cool, dark, quiet room.
  4. 4. A wind-down routine: shower, reading, a short breathing or visualization exercise.
  5. 5. Plan around late training: a light dinner and a calm hour before bed.
  6. 6. Short naps (20–30 minutes) can help on heavy days, but not late in the afternoon.
Before adding another training session, check if your athlete is sleeping enough. It's often the fastest performance gain available.

Recovery is part of training

Rest days, easy weeks and holidays from structured training are not lost time. They are when the body adapts to the work.
