What to Say to Your Child After a Loss (and What Not To)

The car ride home matters more than you think. Simple phrases that help young athletes learn from losses without losing confidence.

TSC Academy coaching team · November 26, 2026 · 2 min read

The final whistle goes and your athlete walks off the court or pitch with their head down. The next 30 minutes, often the car ride home, shape how they will feel about the loss, and about the sport.

Why the car ride home is tricky

Right after a loss, emotions are high and listening is low. Even well-meant feedback ("you needed to move your feet more") can land as criticism. Many young athletes say the car ride home is one of the worst parts of competing. Not because of what happened in the match, but because of what is said afterwards.

Five phrases that help

  • "I love watching you play." Simple, unconditional, and it separates your love from the result.
  • "How are you feeling?" Then listen. Don't fix.
  • "What was one thing you did well today?" It trains the habit of finding something useful, even in a bad day.
  • "Do you want to talk about it now or later?" It gives them control.
  • "I'm proud of how you kept fighting." Praise effort and behavior, things they control.

Phrases to avoid

  • "Why did you…?" in the first hour. It feels like an interrogation.
  • Comparisons with teammates or opponents.
  • Blaming the referee, the coach or the conditions. It teaches them that results are out of their hands.
  • Talking about rankings, selection or scholarships after a single match.
A useful rule: within 24 hours of a match, let your athlete bring up the performance first.

When they want to analyze

Some athletes do want to talk it through. If they ask, keep it to one thing that went well and one thing to work on, and turn it into a plan for practice. "What could we try this week?" is better than "what went wrong?".

The long game

Kids who feel safe after losses are more likely to take risks in matches, try new skills and stay in their sport longer. Your reaction after a loss is part of their training, too.
