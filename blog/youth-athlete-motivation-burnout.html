Losing Motivation: How to Prevent Burnout in Young Athletes

Signs that a young athlete is losing motivation or heading toward burnout, and what parents can change at home.

TSC Academy coaching team · November 21, 2026 · 1 min read

Your athlete used to beg to go to practice. Now you're dragging them out the door. A drop in motivation is common, especially between 11 and 15. Sometimes it's a phase. Sometimes it's an early sign of burnout.

Signs to watch

  • They stop talking about their sport, or only talk about results and pressure
  • Frequent "I'm tired" or small aches before practice
  • Less effort in training, more frustration
  • Loss of enjoyment even when they win
  • Withdrawing from teammates or friends

Common causes

  • Too much, too early. Year-round training in a single sport without breaks.
  • The sport stops feeling like theirs. Goals are set by adults, not by them.
  • Pressure around results, rankings, selection or scholarships.
  • No visible progress, which makes effort feel pointless.
  • Life outside sport is squeezed: friends, school, rest.

What helps

  1. 1. Give them ownership. Ask what they want from the sport this season. Let them set one goal.
  2. 2. Talk about effort and growth, not outcomes.
  3. 3. Build in real breaks. Several weeks a year without structured training in their main sport.
  4. 4. Keep variety. Other sports and free play build better athletes and protect enjoyment.
  5. 5. Make progress visible. Small, measurable improvements every month.
Motivation grows from three things: feeling capable, feeling in control, and feeling connected to the people around the sport.

When it's more than motivation

If low mood, anxiety or changes in sleep and appetite show up outside sport too, talk to your pediatrician or a licensed mental health professional.
