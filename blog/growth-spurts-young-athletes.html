Growth Spurts in Young Athletes: Why They Get Clumsy and How to Adapt Training

During growth spurts young athletes can lose coordination and get sore knees or heels. What is happening and how to adjust training safely.

TSC Academy coaching team · November 25, 2026 · 1 min read

One season your athlete moves well. A few months later they look awkward, slower, maybe complaining of sore knees or heels. Often the reason is simple: they are growing.

What happens during a growth spurt

In the years around puberty, bones can lengthen faster than muscles and tendons adapt. Limbs get longer, the center of mass moves, and the brain has to "re-learn" how the body moves. That is why coordination, timing and even speed can dip for a while. It is normal and usually temporary.

Growth spurts often happen roughly between 10 and 14 for girls and 12 and 16 for boys, but every child is different. A simple way to track it is to measure height every 2–3 months. Fast growth is a signal to adjust training.

Common signs

  • Sudden drop in coordination or technique that used to be automatic
  • Feeling tired more quickly, or needing more sleep
  • Soreness around the knee (below the kneecap) or the back of the heel, especially after jumping and sprinting
  • Tight hamstrings and calves

How to adapt training

  • Keep strength training, lower the load. Body-weight and controlled strength work help the body cope with the change.
  • Work on landing and deceleration. Learning to land softly protects knees and ankles.
  • Watch the volume of jumps and sprints. Reduce high-impact work for a few weeks if soreness appears.
  • Re-train technique patiently. Short, frequent technique drills help the brain update the new body.
  • Protect sleep. Growth and recovery both happen mostly during sleep.
Growth spurts are not the time to chase personal bests. They are the time to build a body that will be ready when growth slows down.

When to see a doctor

Pain that gets worse, pain at night, swelling, limping, or pain that doesn't settle with a few days of reduced load should be checked by a doctor or sports medicine professional. Coaching can adjust training around growth. Diagnosing pain is a medical job.
