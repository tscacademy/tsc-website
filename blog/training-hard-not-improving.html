My Child Trains Hard but Isn't Improving. What's Going On?

Plateaus are common in youth sport. The most frequent reasons young athletes stop improving and what to change.

TSC Academy coaching team · November 22, 2026 · 1 min read

More training, same results. It's one of the most frustrating situations for parents and athletes. The good news: plateaus usually have clear causes, and more volume is rarely the answer.

1. They are doing more of the same

Repeating the same drills at the same intensity builds habits, not improvement. Progress needs practice that is slightly too hard: new variations, decision-making, game-like pressure.

2. A physical limiter nobody is testing

Sometimes technique is fine but the body can't deliver it: not enough strength to change direction, limited mobility in hips or shoulders, poor endurance late in matches. Simple tests show where the real limit is.

3. Not enough recovery

Growth, school, training and competition all compete for energy. Tired athletes don't adapt. Watch sleep, mood and how often they feel heavy or sore.

4. Growth is changing their body

During growth spurts, coordination and speed can dip temporarily. That is not a lack of effort. Training needs to adapt to the new body.

5. The mental side

Fear of mistakes leads to safe, passive play. Athletes who are afraid to fail stop trying the things that would make them better. Confidence and the freedom to make mistakes are part of improvement.

What to do

  • Test, don't guess. Measure a few simple things: speed, jump, strength, endurance, plus how they feel in competition.
  • Pick 2–3 priorities, not ten.
  • Change the type of practice, not only the amount.
  • Protect sleep and rest days.
  • Re-test every month so progress is visible.
If you can't name the one thing your athlete is working on this month, the plan is probably too broad.
